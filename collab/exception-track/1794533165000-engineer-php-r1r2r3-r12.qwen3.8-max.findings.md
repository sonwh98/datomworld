## 2. The residual, accepted

sol is right and the counterexample is entirely my own making. Let B = `::integer/max-bits`
(100000 in these lanes) and T = 2^(B-1). r11's second probe admitted exactly `|rc| < T`, which
made **T itself an admission endpoint** — and T is one ordinary acquisition away from `T-1`, a
count built only from admitted integer-module operations (`integer/sub (integer/shift-left 1
99999) 1`). So reception admitted a legal local Throwable, php's own repaired `integer/add` moved
its count to T, and the *next* reception's doubling — a computation no ownership operation ever
needs — answered `::bit-limit` and reclassified the unchanged local Exception as a
`ForeignException`. That is re-check5's identity defect recreated by the classifier instead of by
the bounds. My r11 justification ("headroom makes crossing the limit unreachable", "no count the
repaired arithmetic itself produces is ever refused") confused reaching the *arithmetic* limit
with reaching the *predicate's own* exclusion, which takes one acquisition. Retracted in full — §5.

**The invariant is now exactly:** *admitted ⟺ both ownership operations can be performed on this
count under this composition's limits.* The classifier computes nothing of its own — every call in
the predicate is a call the ownership machinery itself makes — which is the property whose absence
made the doubling an endpoint.

**Consequence, stated as the dispatch requires:** counts in `[T, 2^B)` that the doubling used to
exclude are now admitted whenever `rc+1` (and `rc-1`) stay integers. Concretely, at B=100000:
`2^99999` (T), `2^99999+1`, `2^100000-2` are all LOCAL now and were FOREIGN under r11, while
`2^100000` and anything wider stay FOREIGN. sol's re-check6 point stands and is now satisfied: T
"is directly constructible by integer/shift-left and supports both ownership operations", so it
must not be rejected.

**What remains, and is not claimed away:** the arithmetic's own ceiling. A count at `2^B - 2` is
admitted, one acquisition makes it `2^B - 1`, and the reception after that refuses it — because
`integer/add` genuinely cannot produce `2^B` under `::max-bits = B`. That is the integer module's
limit surfacing, not a cutoff the classifier chose, and no admission predicate expressed in terms
of that module can do better without the module growing (a `max-bits` export, or a saturating
count policy). Reaching it from a minted count (`:rc 1`) takes `2^B` acquisitions; only a
deliberately constructed count of magnitude ≈2^(B-1) starts within one acquisition of it. **Local
identity is therefore claimed while a count stays operable, not unconditionally** — that is the
corrected form of the claim r11 overstated.

## 6. My one addition beyond the dispatch: the decrement probe

The dispatch said "KEEP the increment probe … and the `integer/integer?` domain check". I kept
both and made the second probe `integer/sub rc 1` instead of removing the slot. Reason: with the
doubling gone, increment-only admission leaves a **release**-side path that stores `::bit-limit`
as `:rc` — the very thing re-check6 forbids ("Do not apply a bare deletion that lets `::bit-limit`
become stored :rc"), and the property sol's own soundness ruling covered ("no bypass that passes
both probes and immediately installs `::bit-limit` during an ordinary **acquisition/release**").

Reachable with module operations only, at B=100000:

```clojure
(let [x (integer/sub (integer/shift-left 1 99999) 1)      ; 2^99999-1, 99999 bits
      y (integer/mul x 2)                                  ; 2^100000-2, 100000 bits (mul's
                                                           ;   pre-check is 99999+2-1, not > B)
      z (integer/add y 1)]                                 ; 2^100000-1, 100000 bits
  (integer/neg z))                                         ; rc = -(2^100000-1)
```

Under increment-only admission this rc is admitted (`rc+1` = -(2^100000-2), 100000 bits, an
integer). The first ordinary **release** then computes `integer/sub rc 1` = -2^100000, 100001 bits
→ `::bit-limit` → the keyword is `assoc`'d into the cell as `:rc`, and the next ownership
operation on it throws `"integer primitive refused"` `{:reason :wrong-type, :op add/sub, :expected
:integer}` — a host failure escaping the boundary. With the decrement probe the same payload is
FOREIGN and retained whole.

This does **not** recreate the doubling's defect, and the distinction is the round's whole lesson:
`integer/sub rc 1` is not a margin the classifier invented, it is literally delref's operation, so
the predicate still computes nothing the ownership machinery does not compute. It moves no cutoff
on the positive side either — `rc ≤ 2^B - 2` is binding there whether or not the decrement is
probed, since for positive `rc` a successful `rc+1` implies a successful `rc-1`. Its only effect is
to refuse counts at the *negative* ceiling, where release genuinely cannot be performed. It is one
pure call on a path that runs once per boundary reception. If sol prefers the literal minimal fix,
deleting that one line is the whole revert — but then the release-side hazard above needs a
different answer, and I could not find one that is not a broad host catch.
