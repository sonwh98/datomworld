# Findings: replayable reply source for the UCF holder over the landed front/reply transport

**Verdict: yes — buildable within D15's composition scope, with one ownership caveat and two guarantees D15 must pin.** The landed front treats its reply stream as an opaque composition-supplied DaoStream writer, and the landed tree already contains a complete-retention, stable-identity, positionally readable transport family (`dao.stream.memory-log` under `dao.stream.journal`, durable via `dao.stream.journal.file` over `dao.jing.file`). What it does **not** contain is any *composition* that uses that family for replies — every landed reply stream today is an evicting ring. That missing wiring is exactly D15's obligation, not a change to landed code.

## 1. Per-substrate retention and read semantics

**`dao.stream.ringbuffer` — NOT conforming.** `/Users/sto/workspace/datomworld/src/cljc/dao/stream/ringbuffer.cljc`. Fixed-capacity, evict-oldest by construction: `append!` computes `first' (max (:first s) (- n (:capacity s)))` and dissocs the evicted position (lines 124-131). A read below `:first` answers `:dao.stream/gap` with a recovery cursor at `:first` (lines 88-98) — the record is gone forever. Identity is a random UUID minted per `fresh-state` (line 27) with no restore path, so it is not stable across process restart. Fails both "complete retention" and "same identity and position after restart".

**`dao.stream.memory-log` — conforming within a process lifetime.** `/Users/sto/workspace/datomworld/src/cljc/dao/stream/memory_log.cljc`. Docstring states it directly: "unbounded, append-only… complete retention… absence of eviction is structural" (lines 1-29). State is one vector dense from 0; `next` is a pure positional read (deref + range check + `nth`, lines 124-147) — it mutates nothing, so it cannot advance a hidden cursor. Positions never exceed the tail; `blocked` at the tail while open, `end` after close (lines 141-147); history below the tail stays readable after close (lines 96-113). Identity is per-process (line 73) *except* through `restore` (lines 191-199), which `dao.stream.journal` uses to keep a persisted identity. So: stable identity and complete retention only when a durable owner (the journal) reconstitutes it.

**`dao.stream.journal` — the conforming substrate.** `/Users/sto/workspace/datomworld/src/cljc/dao/stream/journal.cljc`. "Durable, complete-retention": frame 0 is the header carrying the identity, frame p+1 is `{::position p ::value v}`, p dense from 0; "cursors are the visible log's positions, so both are the same after a reopen of the same frames" (lines 1-13). Position bound 2^52-1 (`max-position`, lines 50-52) — this is the ruling's "journal's existing bound". Reads delegate to a restored memory-log (lines 245-254, 264-273); an uncertain append poisons appends only, "reads keep serving the visible log" (lines 15-23); `open!` replays the header and values, truncating only a torn final frame (lines 276-300). The file backend (`/Users/sto/workspace/datomworld/src/cljc/dao/stream/journal/file.cljc`) stores each frame as one content record in `journal.jing` (a `dao.jing.file` content file, fsync per put) under a directory lock (lines 1-19, 73-96) — i.e., the durable substrate under the reply stream is dao.jing storage. Tri-host (clj/cljs/cljd).

**`dao.stream.remote` + ws — cross-process without losing the properties.** `/Users/sto/workspace/datomworld/src/cljc/dao/stream/remote.cljc`: the mirror serves the entry's own handle and "never constructs, parses or rewrites a cursor… the served stream is the original, its cursors, its positions, its gap, its end" (lines 248-259, 11-15); the reflection's descriptor carries "the same identity as the source's own descriptor" (`refl-descriptor`, lines 568-577); outcomes are returned verbatim (lines 610-616, 220-245). So a holder process reading a served reply journal reads positionally against the stable identity. Channels are `dao.stream.ws` (`/Users/sto/workspace/datomworld/src/cljc/dao/stream/ws.cljc`, host adapters exist under `src/clj|cljs|cljd/dao/stream/ws/`).

## 3. The D15 adapter, and whether anything falls outside its diff

**Buildable as pure composition.** A conforming `:reply-inbox` descriptor is: identity = the reply journal's header identity; `read-at!` = `(fn [n] …)` building the memory-log cursor `{:dao.stream.memory-log/identity i :dao.stream.memory-log/position n}` (or the reflection equivalent) and calling `stream/next`, then mapping `:ok` → `{:status :record :position n :author arb :record v}`, `:blocked`/`:end` at the tail → `:empty`, and `:transport-error`/`gap` → `:unavailable` (a journal never gaps; the outcome algebra needed is already in the handles). D15a supplies the descriptor shape, assembly validation and retention records; D15 supplies the production wiring: open the reply journal (file backend) in the authority's composition, hand the writer to `front/front` (no front.cljc change), and serve it (`dao.stream.remote/mirror-step` over ws) so the holder's side can attach by identity and read positionally after restart.

**Prerequisites / things to report (none is a landed-code change, but two are binding):**
1. **Ownership sequencing, not code**: the production REPL composition of fronts/authorities does not exist yet (M-next D pending). If D15 is expected to land those wiring files, that is consistent with its "composition-only scope" (`collab/1791370000000-…d15-driver-seam-ruling…md:4,81`: "D15 then composes those public functions without modifying holder internals"; the inbox ruling line 4: "D15 owns the production adapters"). No change to `front.cljc`, `driver.cljc`, engine, lease or wire is required for the reply source itself — driver.cljc's seam removal is D15a's authorized delta (ruling section 4, line 107).
2. **D15 must pin the recovery guarantee against the declared durability**: the file journal declares only `:process-crash` — "a power cut right after the first open can lose the file and its identity" (`journal/file.cljc:29-35`); a memory journal declares `:failure-model :none` (`journal.cljc:99-106`). The assembly cannot prove durability from a function (ruling lines 48-50), so the adapter's guarantee rests on the declared durability plus crash tests over kept frames — the pattern already used by `fresh-journal`/`reopened-journal`/`cut-backend` in `driver_test.cljc:490-524`.
3. **Rings and bare memory-logs are disqualified as reply sources**, matching the ruling's "ring subject to overwrite is insufficient" (line 98): rings evict (retention gap = permanently lost record, not `:unavailable`-reducible after the fact), and both mint a fresh identity per process. Tests wanting "simulated process recovery" should use journal/memory-backend over kept frames or an `int-stream`-style integer-position handle (`driver_test.cljc:149-189` was built for exactly that).

## 4. The outcome side — confirmed stable

`OutcomeReader` (`/Users/sto/workspace/datomworld/src/cljc/yin/vm/ucf/authority/admission.cljc:349-409`) is reader-only, identity `(str arb "/outcomes")`, cursors `{::outcomes identity ::position n}` dense from 0 (`:oldest` → position 0, lines 364-376), `next` range-checks `0 ≤ pos ≤ tail` and answers `blocked` at the tail, "never ended" (lines 379-398). Ordering: `:outcomes` is a vector only ever extended by `conj` on the `:yin.k/fenced` fact, in fold order (`/Users/sto/workspace/datomworld/src/cljc/yin/vm/ucf/ledger.cljc:658-667`); the fold is deterministic from the journal, whose record t must equal its frame position at open (`authority.cljc:9-12`, enforced at 131-132), and each committed record is folded in order under the authority lock (`authority.cljc:227-248`). Hence indexing is stable as the ledger grows and across reopen. "Missing history is unavailable" maps exactly: a defective journal refuses `open!` (no authority, no reader), and a poisoned or closed authority makes `projection` nil, so the reader answers `:dao.stream/transport-error` (`admission.cljc:364-376,397-398`; `authority.cljc:161-167`). Authentication is preserved: attribution stays the composition's author-equality check (`front/reply-evidence`), which an inbox adapter's `:author` field feeds unchanged.
