# Report: discovery-order numbering, round 4 revised after the r4 review — claude-fable-5-1

## Summary for the owner

The fourth review found no canonicality counterexample: its own 5,000-state tie-heavy generator, with two renamed and shuffled copies per state, gave 0 root or verdict mismatches, and its independent bijection check found a whole-state automorphism in every passing tie group. What it required were resource-gate and wording fixes, all applied here; the rule, the proof and every pinned root are unchanged.
The one serious defect was ordering: the occurrence-count scan recursed before the depth gate, so one cursor cell inside 3000 nested vectors overflowed the host stack instead of refusing `[:depth]`. The gate is now a bounded iterative scan with an explicit stack, run right after the census gate and before anything recursive; it covers rows, members, literal and structural data and the census. The 3000- and 10000-deep states and a 3000-deep literal now refuse `[:depth]` under `{:depth 3}` and under the default budget; 200 nested vectors number.
The representation estimator had lost 6a's early abort and took 400,012 steps on a 400,000-scalar literal under `{:work 2000}`. It is now iterative and checks its running total against the remaining budget after every node: under `{:work 2000}` the depth scan refuses at its 2001st position and the estimator is never entered; under `{:work 900000}` the scan passes and the estimator aborts after 779 steps. The note now says exactly what the charge bounds: encoded bytes and encoder nodes by a conservative abstract bound, not host heap byte for byte.
The probe's decoder ran 6a's frame validation before the discovery recomputation, so the ordering test proved nothing. It now runs 6a's steps 1–5 with frame validation suppressed, then the recomputation, then the root comparison, then `validate-frames`, as production will at `rows.cljc:570-578`. A wrong numbering plus a reserved-name binding decodes to `:noncanonical-cursor-cells`, the right numbering to `:reserved-name`, and the 2-cycle plus the same binding to `:unresolved-cursor-cells`.
The incomplete-key example was stale; the replacement is `#{#{[A B] [B C] [C A]} #{[D E] [F E] [D F]}}`: equal size, every cell occurring twice, equal incomplete keys, and the two inner sets are not isomorphic. The probe refuses it by clause (i) and classifies it exhaustively as not interchangeable, which makes the clause's necessity concrete. The termination measure is now the pair (unnumbered cells, multiset of queued member sizes) with the three phases spelled out and the distinct-members premise stated.
Refusal figures, as the reviewer asked them stated: in their 5,000-state corpus 4,110 numbered and 890 refused; of the refused, 470 exhaustively interchangeable, 100 demonstrably non-interchangeable, 320 sampled-equal and undecided. My rerun of their script reproduces every one of those counts. The rates (0.95% of random values, 2% of frame-bearing states, 17.8% of the tie-heavy corpus) are generator rates only, not evidence of production coverage either way; small valid shapes such as the 2-cycle refuse, the accepted tradeoff of a refusal-capable carrier.
The reviewer's balanced-alias witness `#{[X X Y Y] [Z W Z W]}` is added to the acceptance list: one root for both enumerations, pinned `5376f133…`, the reviewer's own value. The size estimate is corrected upward (about 280 added lines, not 200) and the "cheapest sound test" and "no sound test without a search" claims are withdrawn.
