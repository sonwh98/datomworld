## Objections to my own cell ruling

1. **Seal cost was glossed over.** `authentic-ref?` recomputes a SHA-256 on every check (`engine.cljc:287-299`, `:261-264`). As I specified it, every `cell/get` and `cell/set!` hashes, and box-all puts that on every variable access. **Amendment:** store the seal in the heap entry at allocation and compare on access, leaving one hash per `cell/new`. Even that may be too slow on CLJS and CLJD, where SHA-256 is software; measure in the spike.

2. **Sealing cells is weaker than I implied.** With closures forgeable, a sealed cell ref protects little today. I still hold the decision, because it keeps one reference discipline and does not widen the hole. But if D7 lands as host types, cell refs should become a host type too and the seal disappears. Slice 1's ref shape is therefore provisional.

3. **The threaded-heap rejection was overstated.** State threading gives something the heap cannot: rollback on re-entry. That is the right semantics for backtracking guests. It stays a valid per-frontend choice, which is why D3 says "baseline", not "only".

4. **One claim in the ruling was under-tested.** I asserted that the four VMs share one effect path from reading. The register and stack VMs do call `engine/handle-effect` (`stack.cljc:441-455`, `register.cljc:469-485`), but slice 1's parity tests are the evidence, not my reading.

5. **No reclamation is a larger gap than "can wait" suggested.** Box-all allocates one cell per reassigned local per activation. A long-running loop calling a function grows `:heap` without bound. It does not block the spike, but it should be the first item after it, ahead of lift/lower.
