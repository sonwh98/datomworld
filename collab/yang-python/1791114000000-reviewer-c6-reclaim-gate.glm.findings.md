**Attack (2), losing-candidate refusals vs later winners: no wrongful bar.** The refusal answers `[proposer proposal-id]` and nothing else — dao.lease's own answer-once keying (holders mint proposal ids; `admissible?`'s rationale, lease.cljc:773-787). ## Rulings on the engineer's decisions

**(a) The proposer must ride a separate authority-authored custody fact; the implemented key on the lease fact is the wrong side.** Exact amendment: UCF 7.7.2/7.7.8 publish

```
{:yin.k/custody      :yin.k/refused
 :yin.k/proposer     P
 :dao.lease/proposal pid}
```

authored by the authority, committed in the same transaction as the plain `:dao.lease/rejected` lease fact; the pair is the refusal record. The lease fact stays verbatim dao.lease (its refusal names no proposer of its own); a lease judge draining the pair records nothing from either (as today), a UCF reader reconstructs `:answered` from `:yin.k/refused` alone keyed `[proposer proposal-id]`, and the fold refuses a rejected lease fact without its refused fact in the same record and vice versa — the exact twin of `::unepoched`. The engineer's claim that this "needs a writer protocol change" is not right: the writer already expands single delivered facts into multi-fact transactions (grant → `[grant, bound]`, lapse → `[lapse, reclaimed]`); it would receive the hook's refusal still carrying `:yin.k/proposer` (that shape is unchanged, and `deliver-authored` is untouched), strip the key, and commit the pair. The code delta is confined to `append-fact!`, `attribute-order` (`:dao.lease/rejected` reverts to `[:dao.lease/status :dao.lease/proposal]`; `:yin.k/refused` is `[:yin.k/custody :yin.k/proposer :dao.lease/proposal]`), `refusal-defect`, and the reconstruction source.

**(b) A failed reclaim refuses the whole open — correct.** An append that answers non-ok during reopen is an *uncertain* append in plan 1.4's sense, and plan 1.4's poison is whole-authority by design; per-occurrence service denial would need a new failure model the substrate does not have. UCF 7.7.8's "grants nothing for that occurrence" is about an *unrecoverable epoch* (governance, outside the protocol), not a transient append failure — and reopen is idempotent: the next reopen replays the truth and converges, exactly as `a-cut-during-reopen-refuses-the-open` proves. Add the retry sentence to 7.7.7/7.7.8 *Restart* (finding 2 iv) so D knows the rule.

**(c) `::max-epoch` is acceptable as a test bound.** It mirrors `::max-exact` exactly (same opt shape, same "tests may lower it" contract, threaded into the fold), the default is pinned to the published 2^52-1 by test, and the fold fails closed on a mismatched reopen (`:epoch-mismatch` whenever the histories diverge) with the rule stated in `open!`'s docstring. A production caller passing it gets a self-consistent, merely lower-bound ledger — the same trust extended to `::max-exact` since C3. No guard needed.

**(d) Writing the epoch-change fact at exhaustion with the unchanged epoch is correct.** It keeps the pairing total — every lapse has exactly one `:yin.k/reclaimed`, so the fold needs no bound-aware exception ("a lapse of a lease at the bound may omit its pair") — it makes the reclaim transaction the single linearization point for "the tenure ended and the epoch is terminal", and it carries UCF's "leaves the epoch unchanged" literally in the bytes. Exhaustion derived from it (an epoch change of a lease bound at the maximum carrying the maximum) is equivalent to 7.7.8's "a lapse recorded for the lease bound at 2^52-1". Amend 7.7.8 to name the fact and this derivation (finding 2 i, iii).

**(e) The adapter contract belongs in `docs/design/dao.lease.md`.** Plan section 5 already designates "dao.lease.md or its rationale: the reclaim adapter contract of 1.3" — and the rationale doc "binds nothing", so the operative text goes in the contract doc: extend *Composition duties* (the "reclaim procedure per subject" bullet or a new one beside it) with plan 1.3's contract — readiness-only reclaim; the revocation's linearization point is the transaction that records `:dao.lease/lapsed` and its accompanying facts; no state claiming a successful revocation leaves the grantor's boundary before that transaction commits; a non-ok writer leaves the lease pending in the judge and the projection unchanged. The `grant.cljc` namespace docstring stays as the code-side statement, and UCF 7.7.8 *Restart* gains a pointer. Every future composer of dao.lease over a durable ledger reads dao.lease.md, not a UCF namespace docstring.
