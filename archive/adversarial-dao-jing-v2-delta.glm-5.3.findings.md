## 3. New defects in the changed lines

**P1 | J3c, plan:404–412 (with plan:395–396) | A verify hop that is allocated but never reaches `requested` leaves its loss completion unroutable — the materialization is orphaned with no completion and no exit, and a phantom completion is published for a get the driver never issued.** Registration under the verify-get id happens only "on `requested`." Reach the window three ways, all ordinary: (a) the writer answers `closed`/`transport-error` at issue — `attempt-unsent`'s undeliverable branch completes the request immediately with its fresh id and `:op :jing/get-content`, before `requested` ever occurred; (b) a hop deferred by `full` (unregistered, since it never reached `requested`) whose order-1 re-attempt on the next step comes back undeliverable; (c) the driver calls `abandon` while a deferred hop holds `:unsent` — `abandon-unsent` completes it with `/abandoned`. In all three, the completion's id matches no record (routing is by id only, as the disambiguation test requires), so the wrapper publishes a `:lost` get completion whose id the driver was never handed, and the `:verify-pending` record stays pending forever — not in `:unsent` (it was consumed), not terminal (undeliverable and `/abandoned` set no terminal), swept by nothing. That is the original J3c defect, one level up: the plan states "a `:lost` on either hop completes the materialization" but its mechanism cannot deliver that in precisely the loss paths. Secondary, same lifecycle: the terminal sweep ("every `:verify-pending` record completes `:lost` … exactly once," plan:410–412) is assigned to no step order, and once a routed completion can complete a record, the sweep needs a stated phase-transition guard against double completion (order 3's `abandon-unsent` can complete a *registered* hop's envelope through the outbox while the sweep would complete the record again). **Fix:** register the record under the get id as soon as `rpc/request!` returns one — `requested`, `pending-request`, and `request-undeliverable` all carry `:dao.stream.rpc/id` — so every hop completion routes; state that any `:lost`/`:error` completion on a registered hop id completes the materialization once, with the record's phase transition as the exactly-once guard; assign the terminal sweep to order 4's terminal guard (order 4 completes all `:verify-pending` as `:lost` instead of issuing); add three tests: undeliverable at issue (scripted `closed` writer), undeliverable on the order-1 re-attempt of a deferred hop, and driver `abandon` during a deferred hop — each asserting exactly one materialize `:lost` completion, no phantom, no residual `:verify-pending`.

**P3 | J1.1 scope-contingent paragraph, plan:572–576 | "Under that route the four in-scope edits — `mem_test`, `dht_test`, the `jing_test` section move, the `file_test` excision — still happen" is wrong for two of the four.** Under the documentation route the observer never leaves `dao.jing`, so `dao.jing.observer` does not exist and the `mem_test`/`dht_test` repoints ("each gains a `[dao.jing.observer :as observer]` require") cannot be performed — their `jing/observer-state` calls keep compiling untouched. Only the section move and the file_test excision happen under that route (needed by J1.2 and J2, as the paragraph's own justification correctly says of those two alone — the justification quietly contradicts the list it defends). Executed literally, the sentence produces a require of a nonexistent namespace. **Fix:** one sentence — under the documentation route only the section move (requiring `dao.jing` for the observer) and the excision happen; the seven repoints exist only on the move-out route.

## Summary

The verify-hop decision is correctly made and correctly argued — the principle it spends is spent knowingly, bounded, and in the pattern the RPC layer already established — and four of the four correction targets landed (one with a fresh error in its contingent branch, P3). What remains is one real execution gap inside the new mechanism: the hop lifecycle is specified for the paths where the hop *sends* and the path where everything *dies*, but not for the path where the hop is allocated and then lost before registration — the exact path where "a `:lost` on either hop completes the materialization" is promised and cannot be delivered. That window is closable with registration-at-issue plus an assigned, guarded terminal sweep, and with it closed I have no further objection to this plan.
