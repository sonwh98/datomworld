## What would change my mind

**C1 (least confident of the rejections).** Evidence that some *other* layer needs cursors over the content file — a durable observer checkpoint design that reads the content log positionally, or a `dao.space` recovery path that replays it — would make the log a stream with a second reader and flip me to option (a) or to re-basing on a future v2 log transport. Also: if the orchestrator reads `datom.world.md:66-68` as condemning DaoJing's handle map *and* wants that fixed now, then the right move is not a v2 append-log but a redesign of `materialize!` as an effect on a stream, which is a different and larger plan; I would concede C1 as "blocked on that" rather than "build the transport".

**C2 (confident on the defect, less on the fix).** If the orchestrator holds the `dao.jing*` scope line absolutely, the five-test-file repoint is out and I fall back to GLM's sentence plus an honest transitive claim. What would make me abandon the move-out entirely: a `src/` consumer of the v1 observer I failed to find. My grep was `observer-state\|observe-step!` over `src` with `--include='*.clj*'`; a caller reaching it through `ns-resolve` or a string would evade it, and I do not believe one exists.

**C3.** Nothing would move me back to raw `request-put`. What could change the *shape*: if a reviewer shows the read-back-verify hop is unnecessary over RPC because `default-handlers` already validates address-against-payload server-side (`remote.cljc:57`) — then `:present` from a conforming server already implies equality, and the second hop guards only against a non-conforming server. I would still keep it, because `materialize!` keeps it locally against a conforming backend for the same reason, but I would accept it being declared optional per composition.

**C4.** Only an argument that `dao.jing.observer` is the wrong final name — e.g. that the observer should fold into `dao.jing` proper because `observer-state` is DaoJing's entire operational surface. I chose the split because it is what the C2 fix produces for free and because it keeps the stream-facing code separable from the address-minting core, which C2 showed is worth having.

**C5, C6.** Repository facts, verified by the orchestrator; nothing would move me. The only open question is the step's ordering — re-attempt unsent *before* or *after* polling — and I chose before, because a `/detached` observed in the poll would then abandon an envelope the same step had just re-attempted; a reviewer showing that ordering produces a duplicate completion would make me reverse it.

**N1.** A ruling that consumer plans may not delete v1 files would end it; I would then choose "leave standing" over "hand off". Evidence that `dao.stream.log` has a consumer I missed — some `(require 'dao.stream.log)` for its `defopen` side effect in a demo or `src/dev` — would also end it; the docket says only three requirers exist and I did not re-derive that.
