Opus's measurement and mechanism are right, and my nit's premise was wrong: the linearizer assigns a fresh register to every value-producing instruction — the goldens corroborate (`(f (g x) y)` → `[:var 1 f] [:var 3 g] [:var 4 x] [:call 2 3 [4] false]`), so `(fn [x] (+ x x))` loads `x` twice into two distinct registers and `layout(p) ++ uses(p)` never repeats a register in any *projected* program — the agreement clause cannot be exercised by any corpus AST, and no seam inside this test can make it. Where the clause is live: the vector grammar's item 10 requires operand vectors to contain "registers only", not *distinct* registers (`code.cljc:644-659`), so a hand-built `[:call r f [x x]]` is well-formed, and that is exactly the shape 6e item 6's hand-built-vector pinning should carry — both the executed ordered-region comparison for such a vector and the real enforcement site, 6e's stack lift, where a repeated register means two region/slice positions that must decode to agreeing values (and divergent values must refuse). So: keep `(is (zero? @repeated))` — it is strictly stronger than the old `<= 0` because it pins the linearizer's no-repeat property as an invariant: a future register-reusing linearizer fails here loudly and forces a conscious replacement of the pin with a genuine repeated-register fixture rather than silently relying on the group-agreement branch — and keep the per-group agreement assertion as is (correct-but-dormant for projected vectors, live the day repeats exist).
