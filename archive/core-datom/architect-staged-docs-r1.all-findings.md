## Reviewer A — gpt-5.6-sol

No Critical/P0 findings. I found six High/P1 and five Medium/P2 findings. No Low/P3 findings.

1. **High/P1 | [dao.stream.md:196](/Users/sto/workspace/datomworld/docs/design/dao.stream.md:196), [dao.stream.ws.md:293](/Users/sto/workspace/datomworld/docs/design/dao.stream.ws.md:293) | `attach!` reports success before its success condition is true.**

WebSocket `attach!`, however, returns `ok` for every locally valid descriptor before the handshake; it may later deposit `:ws/not-found`, proving that no named stream existed and no attachment occurred. Make WebSocket return `pending`, followed by a correlated `opened`, `not-found`, or `transport-error` event. Alternatively, redefine `ok` everywhere as “the local attempt was accepted,” but then remove the guarantees that `attach!` joins the named existing stream.

## Findings, most severe first

**1. High | dao.stream.md:419–426 | `next` has no `transport-error` outcome, so a durable reader transport has no lawful answer to a runtime read failure.**
The Result Convention (line 104) states "transport defects are all outcomes, never exceptions and never callbacks," and the exhaustive-set rule (lines 90–91) says a transport may produce *fewer* outcomes than the table, never any outside it. But the `next` table's set is `{ok, blocked, end, gap, cursor-mismatch, invalid-cursor}` — no `transport-error`. A disk-backed log whose read fails at runtime cannot answer honestly: `gap` lies (gap means evicted, and carries recovery-cursor semantics), `blocked` lies (retrying won't help), throwing is forbidden, and displacement-to-another-channel (Surfaces, lines 355–357) is reserved for answers "only the far end can give" — a local read failure has none. The ws spec itself anticipates durable deposit media whose storage fails (ws:222–224, for `append!`). The contract's own text is internally contradictory here. **Confidence: high.** What would change my mind: an argument that reader-surface transports are definitionally in-memory — but Retention and Gaps (line 517) explicitly names "a file or append-only log" as a retaining transport. **Correction:** add `:dao.stream/transport-error` to the `next` table (and consider `cursor`, whose transport-owned anchors — "a byte offset, a timestamp," line 386 — may also require transport work that can fail).

**2. High | dao.stream.implementation-plan.md:320–324 (with 136–140) | Flow control (4d) depends on a backlog measurement no document provides an operation for.**
4d's premise: "The reader publishes its own backlog. Only it can… Occupancy relative to *your* cursor is private to you. When it passes the declared high-water figure, the reader asks for a pause." But the contract's reader surface is `cursor` + `next`, cursors are opaque transport-owned values with no arithmetic, and `next` returns one value at a time. The only way a reader can learn its distance from the tail through the public surface is to read the backlog — which is catching up, the thing it can't do; and the first surface-level signal of falling behind is `gap`, which arrives after the loss high-water exists to prevent. The contract *permits* a transport-owned operation beside the public surface (lines 334–336) that could answer this, but Phase 2's ring-buffer deliverable list doesn't include one, and 4d never names one. As written, 4d is unimplementable by someone holding these three documents. **Confidence: high.** What would change my mind: a reading of "occupancy" under which the client-side reader can compute it from `next` results alone — I couldn't construct one. **Correction:** add an explicit transport-owned occupancy/lag operation (or an optional key on `next`'s `ok` map, which the open-map rule permits) to the Phase 2 ring buffer deliverables, and have 4d name it.

**3. Med-High | dao.stream.ws.md:80–89 vs dao.stream.md:106 | The ws spec mints `:dao.stream/attachment` inside the contract's namespace, and the contract's own rule forces it to.**
Contract line 106: "All qualified keywords in results live under `:dao.stream/…`." So a transport-owned result key (`:ws/attachment`) is forbidden — and the ws spec, needing attachment identity in the `attach!` result, therefore defines a new key in the *contract's* reserved vocabulary that the contract never defines. That is subordinate overreach with a concrete failure mode: nothing stops a second transport minting a colliding `:dao.stream/*` key with different semantics, and a contract-only reader has no way to know `:dao.stream/attachment` exists (the ws spec itself says "keep it — nothing else identifies this attachment," ws:133). Attachment identity is also not ws-specific — any attaching transport with a shared deposit medium needs it, and Phase 5's fact 3 tests it as if it were general. **Confidence: high** that the namespace collision exists; **medium** on the right fix. **Correction:** promote `:dao.stream/attachment` into the contract as an optional standard key on `attach!`'s `ok` map (mirroring how `:dao.stream/shibi` is reserved), or amend line 106 to permit transport-namespaced result keys and move the key to `:ws/…`.

**4. Medium | dao.stream.md:81–82 | The claim "Every clause below derives from these invariants" is false for several load-bearing clauses, and the claim is used as an admission test.**
Not derivable from the ten as stated: cursor provenance / no-`seek` ("every valid cursor comes from the stream" — no invariant forbids fabricated positions; the Explicitly Absent section justifies `seek`'s absence from this clause, not from an invariant, which is circular); open result maps (extensibility appears in no invariant); the *exhaustiveness* of outcome sets ("outcomes are data" does not entail the vocabulary is closed); descriptors-never-carry-code (lines 199–201 — a security/portability decision from no invariant); idempotent `close!`. This matters beyond pedantry because lines 81–82 make derivability the test for future additions: an overstated claim makes the test unenforceable. **Confidence: high** under a strict reading; another reviewer may defend a loose reading of "derives," which I'd contest but note. **Correction:** either add the missing invariants (cursor provenance, closed outcome vocabulary, envelopes-are-inert-data) to the list of ten, or soften the claim to "derives from or is compatible with."

**5. Medium | dao.stream.implementation-plan.md:308–348 | 4d invents wire-visible protocol with no specification home — and puts liveness pings in the wrong layer.**
The pause lease (duration, renewal, caps) travels on the socket and must be recognized by the serving side: that is interoperable vocabulary, exactly what the ws spec's Handshake section says compositions must not invent privately (ws:274–281). Yet the ws spec's Deferred list (ws:360) declares backpressure "outside both this transport and the contract," and the plan is transient — "consumed as the phases complete." When the plan is consumed, the lease protocol is specified nowhere. Separately, "the serving side pings on an interval and drops a connection after two unanswered" is WebSocket wire mechanics — by the plan's own layering rule ("the handshake's wire vocabulary is not composition; it belongs to 4a," plan:301) liveness belongs to 4a/the ws spec, not to a flow-control deliverable, and 4d's opening claim "Nothing here is a transport change" is thereby wrong. **Confidence: medium-high.** **Correction:** move ping/liveness into `dao.stream.ws.md` (at minimum its Deferred list), and give the lease vocabulary a durable spec home — a section of the ws spec or a sibling protocol document — before 4d is built.

**6. Medium | dao.stream.implementation-plan.md:195–199 vs dao.stream.md:328–330 | The server-side accepted-connection handle's `descriptor` answer is unspecified though the contract mandates it.**
The contract: "every handle answers [`descriptor`]… `descriptor` has no failure outcome." The plan mints server-side handles from accepted sockets ("neither `create!` nor `attach!` produces it") — writer+closable, on the outbound path to one client. What descriptor does it return? The served stream's? Something naming the connection (which the ws spec says a descriptor never names, ws:9)? Neither subordinate says, and an implementer must invent it in Phase 4a. **Confidence: high** that it's unspecified; **medium** that it's more than a one-line fix. **Correction:** state in the ws spec that a server-side connection handle's `descriptor` names the served stream the connection joined (the natural answer, since identity is logical-stream identity), or explicitly carve out what else it names.

**7. Medium | dao.stream.ws.md:73–75 vs 314–315 | "No attaching caller chooses or knows [the deposit medium]" vs "it reads the answer from the medium instead of from a return value."**
The Duplex Model says the deposit destination "is not a parameter of `attach!`, and no attaching caller chooses or knows it." Operations says a caller's retry policy "reads the answer from the medium." Both cannot hold as written: to read the resolution event correlated by its attachment id, the caller must hold a reader handle on the very medium it "does not know." The resolution is presumably that the *composition* wires both the boundary and the retrying interpreter — but the text asserts the caller's ignorance and the caller's reading in the same document without connecting them. **Confidence: medium** — this may be a wording defect rather than a design defect, which is what round 2 should settle. **Correction:** replace "no attaching caller chooses or knows it" with "it is not a parameter of `attach!`; the composition that wires the boundary also hands the medium to whichever interpreter owns retry policy."

**8. Low-Med | dao.stream.implementation-plan.md:109–117 vs dao.stream.md:352–357 | The conformance harness consumes surface declarations but not outcome declarations, and no document gives the outcome declaration a data form.**
The contract requires a transport to declare "which outcomes it produces, and for each one it excludes, why," with three admissible reason classes — and warns "an exclusion with no stated reason is an unimplemented outcome." The Phase 1 harness takes "factories plus its declared surface set" only. So the contract's declaration requirement is checkable nowhere, and a third-transport author must invent the declaration's representation (the deposit-admission declaration in *Settled before Phase 1* is a separate, narrower datum). **Confidence: medium-high.** **Correction:** have the harness also take the per-operation produced-outcome sets as data and assert observed outcomes ⊆ declared ⊆ contract table.

**9. Low-Med | dao.stream.md:423, 470–479 | `end` on a closed *attachment* to a still-live stream: what bounds "exhausted" is unspecified.**
After an attachment closes, `next` "answers `end` … once observation through this handle is exhausted." The logical stream is still open and growing. Does the closed attachment's handle observe values appended after the attachment closed (retained history keeps extending), or is "exhausted" bounded by a snapshot at close time? Either answer is defensible; a conformance suite must pick one, and Phase 3's attach-via-directory ring buffer will hit it. **Confidence: medium.** **Correction:** one sentence in Close fixing the bound (I'd argue: a closed attachment observes nothing appended after its close — otherwise "attachment" has no observational meaning for local transports).

**10. Low | dao.stream.implementation-plan.md:268–271 | Dangling cross-reference: "see the open items."**
The parenthetical about respecifying `forward!` as a step function cites "the open items"; no such section exists in the document. Worth noting beyond the broken link: the step-function alternative is the one that better satisfies "no implicit control flow" (a self-rescheduling loop is a callback chain wearing a scheduler argument), and the plan should decide it rather than parenthesize it. **Confidence: high** on the dangling reference; **low-medium** on pressing the design point — I'd yield if the loop form is defended as the driver's concern.

**11. Low | dao.stream.ws.md:287–315 | The successful connection-resolution event is never named in Operations.**
`:ws/not-found` and `:ws/transport-error` are specified as resolution events; the success case is only inferable from `:ws/opened` in the envelope comment (ws:99). One sentence fixes it. **Confidence: high.**

## The three things I am least sure of (round 2 focus)

1. **Finding 3's correction direction.** The collision is real, but whether attachment identity belongs in the contract (my lean) or the namespace rule at contract:106 should be relaxed is a genuine design fork with consequences for every future transport.
2. **Finding 4's strictness.** If the team reads "derives" as "is consistent with and motivated by," most of my counterexamples dissolve — but then the admission test at lines 81–82 has no teeth, which I think is the actual defect. I'd hold the finding but accept the softer correction.

# Round 1 findings — DaoStream v2 staged docs

## Findings, most severe first

**1. HIGH | plan:205–206 (against ws:283–284, plan:350–376) — Phase 4a implements a handshake the ws spec explicitly declares nonexistent, and no phase ever settles it.**
Plan 4a says: "Handshake: encode and recognize the served-stream presentation and the authoritative disclaimer *specified in `dao.stream.ws.md`*." The ws spec says of exactly those things: "Their exact form is **not specified yet**; see Deferred. Until it is, two independently written implementations cannot interoperate and no wire-level conformance test can be written" (ws:283–284). Phase 5 requires two processes to interoperate. The same gap covers the value codec (ws:355–356) and the close code (ws:351–354). The plan's own Phase 3 decision gate (plan:174–180) exists precisely because "provisional keys carried into a wire format under delivery pressure become permanent by accident" — and then applies that logic only to descriptor keys, not to the handshake, disclaimer, close code, or codec, which are equally load-bearing wire format. As written, an implementer entering 4a must invent the transport's core protocol mid-phase.
**Confidence: high.** Direct textual contradiction; grep-verifiable.
**Correction:** Add a decision gate at the Phase 3/4 boundary mirroring the descriptor gate: settle handshake presentation, disclaimer form, the application-range close code, and the value codec in `dao.stream.ws.md` before 4a begins; rewrite the 4a handshake bullet to "specified in `dao.stream.ws.md` *after the Phase 3/4 gate*."

**2. HIGH | contract:419–426 (Reading table) and 392–394 (`cursor` set) — the read-side outcome sets omit `:dao.stream/transport-error`, contradicting the contract's own Result Convention.**
The Result Convention states: "transport defects are all outcomes, never exceptions and never callbacks" (contract:101–105), and the exhaustive-set rule forbids any outcome outside the table. Yet the Reading table contains exactly `ok, blocked, end, gap, cursor-mismatch, invalid-cursor` — no `transport-error` — while `create!`, `attach!`, and `append!` all carry it. The contract itself names durable transports in scope: "a file or append-only log retains everything" (contract:518–519). A file read can fail at `next`. Under the exhaustive-set rule that failure can be reported as *nothing* — not an exception (forbidden), not `gap` (that means eviction, and reporting it would be a lie). Same class, lower confidence: `close!`'s set is `{ok}` (contract:459–461), so a durable transport whose close must flush can never report a flush failure. The defect propagates: plan 4b requires `forward!` to be "total over the outcome algebra — every outcome of `next` and of `append!`" and then lists source branches for `cursor-mismatch` and `invalid-cursor` only (plan:275–280) — the plan inherited the omission. A conformance suite built "exactly as the contract's tables state" (plan:100–102) would encode it permanently.
**Confidence: medium-high.** Falsifiable: point to the derivation of read-failure reporting for a file transport under the current tables. What would change my mind: contract text scoping the Reading table to media that cannot fail, or an author statement that read failures are intended to surface as something other than an outcome of `next`.
**Correction:** Add a `:dao.stream/transport-error` row to the Reading table; decide and state whether `cursor` carries it; decide `close!` (either add the outcome or add one sentence asserting close is definitionally local and best-effort, which is defensible). Add the corresponding source branch to plan 4b's totality list.

**3. MEDIUM-HIGH | plan:246 vs ws:180–185 and plan:304–306 — three owners for one close, and the wrong one is assigned.**
Plan 4b lists "`end` closes the destination" as one of `forward!`'s three owned disciplines. But the ws spec requires that when a served stream ends, connections are closed "with a distinguishing close code" (ws:180–185) so clients deposit `:ws/ended` (stop) rather than `:ws/closed` (reattach) — it calls this distinction the entire point (ws:175–178) and the close code "the fast path for the common case" (ws:204–205). `forward!` is deliberately transport-agnostic; `close!` on a ws handle "means **disconnect from the server**" (ws:328–331). Whether the closing frame carries the ended-code is contextual knowledge (stream ended vs. lease-cap drop vs. endpoint shutdown) that only the composition has — and plan 4c already assigns "close behavior: what a server-side owner closing the logical stream does to connected attachments" to the composition (plan:304–306). As written, if `forward!` unconditionally closes, the common case silently degrades to the slow path: client reattaches, handshake disclaims, client learns late.
**Confidence: medium-high.** What would change my mind: ws spec text defining server-side `close!` to carry the distinguishing code when the served stream has ended — nothing currently says that.
**Correction:** Remove "`end` closes the destination" from 4b's unconditional disciplines; replace with "`end` stops the forwarder and reports" (or make the end-action an argument). 4c closes the connection with the distinguishing code.

**4. MEDIUM-HIGH | contract:81–82 vs contract:194–196, 240–241, 474, and 344–348 — the "every clause derives from these invariants" claim fails for the identity clauses, which are among the most load-bearing in the document.**
None of the ten invariants (contract:64–79) mentions identity, descriptors, or portability. Yet below them the contract asserts: identity "is universal and outlives any attachment, so `descriptor` has no failure outcome" (194–196); "the same descriptor attaches to the same stream every time" (240–241); "`ok` — identity outlives any attachment" (474). Ask which of the ten yields these — none does. Likewise the Surfaces clause "A deposit destination must admit every valid boundary event… wiring one that can refuse is a host assembly defect" (344–348) is imported from the authority's Host Boundaries section, not derived from the ten. These aren't corners: Phase 3's entire round-trip deliverable and ws's ended-stream semantics rest on the identity guarantees. As a governance rule ("a proposed addition that cannot be derived… does not belong"), the claim would reject a legitimate future identity clause or accept these by silent exception.
**Confidence: medium-high.** Falsifiable: produce the derivation for any one of the three cited clauses.
**Correction:** Add an eleventh invariant, e.g.: "A logical stream's identity is plain data, universal across handles, stable across attachment lifecycles and serialization; projecting it never fails." Derive the descriptor clauses from it. For deposit admission, either derive it or cite the authority's Host Boundaries explicitly as its source.

**5. MEDIUM (high confidence) | plan:211 and plan:369–370 vs ws:84–85, 98 — the plan and the ws spec contradict each other on the deposited-envelope key.**
The ws spec is explicit: the attach! *result* carries `:dao.stream/attachment`, while "every event deposited for that attachment carries the same value under `:ws/attachment`" (ws:84–85; envelope at 98) — with a stated rule for why the namespaces differ. The plan says the adapter deposits "as `:dao.stream/attachment`-tagged envelopes" (plan:211) and Phase 5 fact 3 checks that deposited events carry "`:dao.stream/attachment`" (plan:369–370). One of these is wrong, and the ws side is the principled one.
**Confidence: high.** Direct contradiction.
**Correction:** Plan 4a and Phase 5 fact 3: deposited envelopes are `:ws/attachment`-tagged; `:dao.stream/attachment` appears only in the `attach!` result map.

**6. MEDIUM | ws:49 (against contract:283, 142–146, 352–357) — the ws `attach!` exclusion of `transport-error` is overbroad: its stated reason covers only remote failures.**
Ws excludes `transport-error` from `attach!` because it "need[s] a remote answer." But the contract's `transport-error` means "the handler failed" (contract:283), and a handler can fail synchronously and locally — statically calling `ws/attach!` on a host with no socket API, a host constructor throwing on a structurally valid descriptor, local resource exhaustion. That answer is knowable at call time; the contract's "no operation waits… It is a question about now" (142–146) requires returning what is true now, and Surfaces licenses displacement only for "a transport that cannot know a remote answer at call time" (352–357). Displacing a locally-knowable failure is not covered by any of the three honest reasons — by the contract's own standard, "an exclusion with no stated reason is an unimplemented outcome, not a declaration."
**Confidence: medium-high.**
**Correction:** Ws `attach!` produces `transport-error` for synchronously-detected local handler failures; the exclusion narrows to `not-found` and remote-answer `transport-error`, each displaced with its own reason.

**7. MEDIUM | ws:233–242 (against datom.world.md:66–79 and contract:363–366) — teardown-on-failed-deposit departs from the adapter charter, and neither document acknowledges the departure.**
The authority: the adapter "appends one plain-data event and returns. It invokes nothing"; entries "cannot invoke anything" (datom.world.md:66–79). The contract: the adapter's "entire job is to `append!` what arrived" (363–366). The ws spec: on any non-`ok` deposit, the adapter "tears the connection down" (233–242) — a control action, taken in callback context, on the adapter's own judgment of a result. The ws spec's accounting paragraph (251–257) is honest that this corner is imperfect, but nowhere is it admitted that the action exceeds the charter, and it is non-derivable from the ten invariants (see finding 4). I think the choice is probably *right* — the alternatives are silent dropping or an unobservable boundary — which is exactly why it should be a stated rule rather than a silent deviation.
**Confidence: medium.** Expect pushback: "a boundary that can no longer deposit is no longer a boundary; closing its own socket is not invoking." What would change my mind: an explicit derivation showing teardown consistent with the charter as written.
**Correction:** Add to the contract's Surfaces (or the ws admission section) a stated boundary mandate: "a boundary that can no longer deposit must make itself observably gone; the permitted action is closing the socket the boundary itself holds; all other actions are forbidden." The ws rule then derives from it.

**8. MEDIUM | plan:269 — dangling cross-reference to a nonexistent "open items" section, deferring an architecturally live question.**
"(If `forward!` is respeced as a single step returning its next state rather than a loop that reschedules itself — **see the open items** …)". Grep confirms "open items" occurs exactly once in all three documents: this pointer. There is no such section. The deferred question is not cosmetic: it determines who owns rescheduling, and it determines how 4d's stop/restart is implemented — plan:262–267 promises "a stopped forwarder makes no calls at all," but a loop that reschedules itself through the scheduler needs a cancellation mechanism the plan never names.
**Confidence: high** on the dangling reference; medium on the recommendation.
**Correction:** Add the open-items section and resolve the shape. My recommendation: the single-step form — stop/start then collapses into "the driver stops calling it," exactly as the parenthetical itself notes, and no cancellation mechanism is needed.

The contract covers post-close behavior of existing handles and says attachments may "rejoin later" (459–486), and implies a kept cursor "resumes through a newly attached handle" (477–479) — but never says whether a fresh `attach!` to an ended stream is `ok` (to read retained history) or `not-found`. Ws answers implicitly (disclaimer → not-found); a reader-capable remote transport implementer must invent it. One sentence in Close fixes it.

## Where I found nothing

Against the authority directly: the four axioms and six non-negotiable invariants are structurally honored throughout — no registry, no callbacks above the adapter, layering is one-way, no graphs — with the single strain point of finding 7. The IO Model's "no operation waits" is consistent with all seven operations *in this slice*; the one silence (asynchronous `create!`) is acknowledged and out of slice, though the plan's ruling ("the handle reports acquisition state through its own operations," plan:52–58) references a mechanism the contract never defines. The plan's 4a/4b/4c/4d decomposition is fundamentally sound — transport, generic interpreter, composition, above-stream policy are the right seams; my quarrels are with two misplaced clauses (findings 3, and the high-water nit), not the layering.

## The three things I am least sure of

1. **Finding 2 (Reading table lacks `transport-error`).** The slice never trips it — every reader surface in scope is an in-memory ring buffer that cannot fail — so an author could argue the tables bind only what transports declare and the gap is presently harmless. My counter is that the contract explicitly names file and log transports and the conformance suite will freeze the tables; but I would withdraw to low severity if the authors state the omission is deliberate and scoped.
2. **Finding 3 (who closes on stream end).** If the intended design is a server-side `close!` that consults its own transport state and carries the distinguishing code when the served stream has ended, the finding dissolves — nothing in either document says that, but nothing forbids it either. This is the finding most likely to be settled by one sentence of author intent rather than by argument.
3. **Finding 7 (teardown vs "invokes nothing").** I hold the *documentation* point firmly — an unacknowledged deviation from the authority's absolute wording ("It invokes nothing") should be stated as a rule — but I hold the *design* point weakly and would accept the rebuttal that closing one's own socket is not "invoking" in the authority's sense. Whether this is a defect or merely missing prose is what round 2 should settle.
