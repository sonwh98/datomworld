## A. Ruling on the author's slices 1–5

**Slice 1 (CBOR + DRDS in `dao.jing.dht.node`) — right problem, wrong home.** Architectural defect in the design, not just a gap: as specified it grows a *second* fragmentation implementation beside the one that already exists and is cross-host. `fragment-envelope` (`src/cljc/dao/stream/udp.cljc:151-157`) is private, and the machinery around it — chunk sizing (`udp.cljc:168-195`), oversize refusal (`udp.cljc:225-233`), bounded reassembly (`udp.cljc:316-361`), source-address-only replies (`udp.cljc:373-399`) — is only reusable for free if the DHT *rides* `dao.stream.udp` as its datagram layer. My ruling on the fork the brief names (weak point 5): **implement `IDhtNet` over a `dao.stream.udp` port** (`make-port` + `send-to!`, `udp.cljc:202-261`), with per-host socket seams feeding `receive!` — exactly the adapter contract its nsdoc states. This satisfies stream primacy (axiom 1, `docs/design/datom.world.md:16`; owner invariant), reuses the 1200-byte figure the DHT itself contributed (`udp.cljc:58-62`), makes slice 3 two thin seams instead of three peers, and makes the browser a channel swap (ws §3.1 / pair relay §3.3) instead of a new transport. The full mirror/link machinery of `dao.stream.remote` §2 is *not* needed — the DHT keeps its own rpc correlation and client-owned retries; only the channel (§3.2) is used.

**On the CBOR switch (weak point 3): justified, but by the transport decision, not by fragmentation.** `dao.stream.transit` is portable (clj/cljs/cljd branches, `transit.cljc:6-10`), so portability does not force it; but §3.2 pins "one CBOR value per datagram, canonical profile," and the pinned canonical codec (`dao.jing.cbor`) is the profile every Jing boundary already shares. Scope: wire only — `IDhtNet` (`dht.cljc:53-86`) and the store handle unchanged; Base64-in-application-value stays per `dao.jing.cbor.md:464-471`; the clean break with no negotiation (`dao.jing.cbor.md:496-503`) is acceptable only because no fleet exists — it must be slice 1 or never.

**Integrity/limits:** agree per-fragment hashes are unnecessary — whole-segment verification is `accept-bytes` (`dht.cljc:167-180`) plus ingress re-verify (`node.cljc:114-127`). One consequence the author does not draw: fragmentation *creates* the amplification vector. Today's worst case is ~1200 bytes of reply for ~100 bytes of request (~12×); after slice 1 it is 64 KiB across ~60 datagrams (~60×). See slice 4.

**Slice 2 (async outbox) — defect correctly identified; contract as written is contradictory (weak point 1).** `dao.stream.memory-log` is process-lifetime by its own docstring: "Process-lifetime, not durable across restart… Durability is dao.jing's job, not this transport's" (`memory_log.cljc:3-6`). So "an acknowledgement means *durably stored locally and queued*" is half-false: the local half is durable only if `:local` is `dao.jing.file`, and the queue half is never durable. Resolution: **weaken the contract, do not durable-ify the outbox.** The local store already *is* the durable record of what exists; because replication is content-addressed and idempotent (`dao.jing.dht.md:83-86`), replication intent is re-derivable from it, and a restart-time re-enqueue sweep can recover lost intent. The honest sentence: *the acknowledgement is the local `:inserted`/`:present` verdict; replication is asynchronous, best-effort, and carries no durability guarantee for in-flight intent.*

Two gaps in the author's mechanism. (a) **The put path also runs `lookup`** (`dht.cljc:214-215`) — iterative `find-closer` round trips, each bounded 500 ms × 3 tries (`node.cljc:236-241`). "Appends the *target address* to the outbox" implies peer targets computed at put time, which leaves the blocking lookup on the round's path. The outbox must carry **content addresses** and defer peer resolution to the runner. (b) The runner is unspecified ("a runner state"); it must be explicit handle state stopped by `close-fn` (`dht.cljc:255-278`), using the repo's injectable-schedule pump precedent (`dao.jing.md:601-608`) or the caller-stepped cadence (`dao.stream.remote.md` §2.4 Drain).

**"Queues or drops" with empty bootstrap (weak point 2) — unresolved, and my ruling is: neither, in local-only mode there is no queue.** A memory-log never evicts ("this namespace contains no expression that removes an element", `memory_log.cljc:7-9`), so queueing for a deliberately-off network grows without bound for a session's life. Network off ⇒ do not enqueue; network on ⇒ enqueue and let the runner drop after bounded attempts, with a counter. Related architectural point: "local-only DHT" should be a **solo `IDhtNet`** (no socket, `known-peers []`, `lookup []`), not a UDP node with empty `:bootstrap` — `create-node` binds a socket and starts a daemon receiver thread regardless (`node.cljc:308-332`). That is what makes owner policy (2) real rather than nominal.

**Slice 3 (non-JVM peers) — gap real, shape wrong.** `dao.stream.remote.md:386-387` already names the host primitives (clj `DatagramSocket`, Node `dgram`, cljd `RawDatagramSocket`). Under the slice-1 ruling, slice 3 collapses to two thin host seams feeding `receive!`; the author's three independent peers would triple the wire code and fork the codec story. Browser deferral: agree, and note the chosen transport is what makes the browser path a channel swap later.

**Slice 4 (hardening) — required, under-sequenced, under-specified (weak point 4).** Because slice 1 creates the amplification vector, the cookie/rate-limit *design* is a slice-1 gate; only the implementation may wait, and nothing may be exposed beyond loopback before it lands. The mechanism the author leaves unspecified: a Kademlia/BEP-41-style cookie — the receiver issues, via a prior `:ping` from the datagram's source, `hash(source ip:port, nonce, time-bucket)`; any request whose reply will fragment (or any store burst) must carry a cookie obtained from that same source within the window. Replies already go to the datagram source (`node.cljc:190-195`; `udp.cljc:385`), so this closes blind-spoof amplification. Rate limiting needs bounded memory (adversaries rotate source ports): LRU-capped token buckets plus a per-source partial-reassembly cap. I would add the **dead-peer shortlist** fix here (`dao.jing.dht.md:180-184`) — the epic's own read path (foreign manifest fetch) exercises it, and under partition every missing address can cost k × 1.5 s.

**Slice 5 (yin.repl integration) — direction right, three omissions.** (1) Unlisted external dependency: the spec mechanism it extends is the durable-store epic's slice 1–3 work, not yet implemented (ground truth 3). (2) Directory lock: a `dht:<dir>` store's local half is `dao.jing.file` in that dir, so the exclusive lock (durable findings §3) still applies — but two REPL processes sharing one DHT must use *different* dirs and bootstrap to each other; say so, or "DHT = shared storage" invites two processes on one dir and a corrupted file log. (3) Local-only = solo net (above). What is published and the read path are already correct in outline: the indexer already drains its intake through a generic `content-store` (`yin/repl/index.cljc:148-160,218-229`), and `get`'s verify-and-cache is `dht.cljc:227-252`.

**Root discovery (author item 5 / weak point 6) — conclusion right, substance missing, and it does not belong in this epic.** "A `dao.stream` of root facts over `dao.stream.remote` rendezvous" is a label, not a design: naming is unimplemented (discovery is proposal-only), writer identity is unauthenticated until ShiBi (peer names are self-certifying but nothing verifies them, `dao.stream.remote.md:503-506`) — unsigned root facts are spam-able with valid-but-foreign manifests, and content addressing gives integrity, not *correct selection*. Retention and reader pinning are likewise open. Crucially, this epic's stated goal does not need it: `<dir>/HEAD` (durable findings §2) *is* root discovery for the single-owner persistent store. Rule: agree it stays outside the DHT; it is a separate epic blocked on discovery + ShiBi, and slice 5 must not wait on it.

## B. The three owner-policy questions

**(1) Default stays `mem`; `dht:` explicit — agree.** A default `dht:` would bind sockets and emit traffic from a bare REPL start (least surprise), the DHT has no GC/pinning so a default would grow unbounded caches (`dao.jing.dht.md:172-174`), and tests stay deterministic. Recommendation: once the durable epic lands, document three postures — `mem` (default), `file:<dir>` (recommended durable), `dht:<dir>` (networked opt-in).

**(2) Network OFF by default even with `dht:` — agree, concretized.** `dht:<dir>` with no configured peers = solo net: no socket, no receiver thread, no outbox appends. "Network on" is the explicit act of naming bootstrap peers — and when a socket actually opens, the REPL banner should announce the bound host:port (a listening socket is a security-relevant side effect the user should see; note the current default bind is loopback, `node.cljc:308`). Never queue replication intent while offline (unbounded growth, above).

**(3) No sensitive REPL data on the open DHT until ShiBi — agree, and enforce it structurally, not by documentation.** Define "sensitive" concretely: *everything the indexer publishes is the user's evaluated source code plus provenance* (session token, root, round ride the metadata entity, `yin/repl/index.cljc:111-115`; `yin.repl.dao.space-index.md:45-51`). Publication is **irreversible** — no deletes by design (`dao.jing.dht.md:13`), replication lands content on k strangers' disks, the network is world-readable, and content addressing delivers integrity, never confidentiality (`dao.stream.remote.md` §1, §7). Enforcement: with bootstrap but no publish opt-in, the store is fetch/cache/serve-only; *originating* publication of locally indexed content requires a second explicit flag (e.g. `--dht-publish`). A documentation-only rule will not survive an eager user. (If the owner insists on one flag, then bootstrap ⇒ full participation must at minimum print exactly what is about to be shared and to whom it will replicate.)

## C. Decide before implementation vs per slice

**Before any code:** (1) the transport fork — `dao.stream.udp`-based `IDhtNet` vs raw per-host peers — it determines slices 1, 3, and the browser path, and the author buried it as incidental; (2) the ack contract sentence (local-verdict ack; non-durable, re-derivable replication intent); (3) local-only semantics (solo net; no enqueue offline); (4) exposure posture (loopback-only until hardening; the amplification budget named at slice-1 design time); (5) codec freeze (the CBOR clean break is only free before a fleet exists).
**Per slice:** cookie construction and rate parameters; runner mechanics and schedule injection; `dht:` spec syntax and flag names; where the solo-net constructor lives; doc updates.

## Where I DISAGREE with the author, explicitly

1. **Outbox ack contract** — "durably stored locally and queued" is contradictory in a `memory-log`; fix the contract wording, not the medium.
2. **Slice 1's home** — building fragmentation inside `dao.jing.dht.node` duplicates `dao.stream.udp`; the right shape is `IDhtNet` over a `dao.stream.udp` port.
3. **"Slices 1–4 run concurrently"** — they cannot (shared files, real dependencies S1→S2/S3→S4).
4. **Hardening as an unconstrained late slice** — its design gates slice 1, because fragmentation is what creates the ~60× amplification vector.
5. **Root-facts item** — a label, not a design, and unnecessary for this epic (HEAD already covers its goal); it should be removed from this epic's slice list.
6. **Missed defect** — the synchronous `lookup` on the put path: an outbox of precomputed peer targets still blocks the REPL round; enqueue addresses.
7. **Missed dependency** — slice 5 extends a startup spec mechanism that does not exist yet (durable-store epic).
8. **"Local-only DHT" as empty bootstrap** — nominally off but still binds a socket and spawns a receiver thread; use a solo net.

Where I agree and add nothing: the five blockers and their evidence; per-fragment hashes unnecessary; local-first durable insert + best-effort replication; root discovery outside the DHT; `mem` default; network-off default; no-REPL-data policy; browser deferral via a remote channel.
